# Berlin trial trace: gpt-5.6-terra

Trace mode, 17 September 2026. This trace used the five commitments fixed in `data/trial/berlin/selection.json`. It did not inspect the other model's trace or report.

The captured coalition agreement identifies the parties as CDU and SPD and is titled for 2023–2026. Its first page says it is the agreement of "CDU Berlin und SPD Berlin" and its title page says "Koalitionsvertrag 2023-2026". This confirms the trial's governing parties and term.

## Results by promise

### Steel boats for the Water Police

**Status: announced.** The Senate's 16 May 2025 answer says that no steel boat was procured in either 2024 or 2025 for lack of funds, and that Police Berlin was only in a Europe-wide market-research phase for a possible procurement.

The done-when is one newly procured steel boat in each year from 2024. That is the most direct reading of "ab 2024 jährlich". It is an ongoing annual obligation, not a target completed by a single plan or budget line. The available evidence establishes missed 2024 and 2025 purchases, then preparation, but not a 2026 order, delivery, or acceptance. CDU's programme promised modern police boats for the Water Police; the SPD programme capture did not yield a matching commitment.

The decisive source is PARDOK written question 19/22511. It is unusually clear because the question quotes the coalition commitment and asks for each year's purchase status. A reviewer should check the 2026/27 budget execution, procurement notices, and any acceptance record before publishing, rather than infer a boat purchase from a budget title alone.

### New law on administrative task allocation

**Status: implemented.** Drucksache 19/2353 says the new Landesorganisationsgesetz would replace the AZG and rules on responsibilities and task demarcation. The gazette records the Verwaltungsstrukturreformgesetz dated 10 July 2025 and its entry into force on 1 January 2026.

The done-when is a law making that replacement, which follows the commitment's specific legal action. The wording admits a secondary reading that the replacement must include every operational detail of the old Zuständigkeitskatalog. The law's transitional provisions therefore matter, but the primary legal replacement has happened. CDU's programme called for clear responsibilities between Senate and districts; SPD's called for clear assignment of tasks, competences, and responsibility.

The decisive evidence is the bill and the official gazette, not a press release. PARDOK supplies the bill as a PDF; the gazette is a PDF with the enacted text. A reviewer should examine the transitional rule and the participation record of the Rat der Bürgermeister before publication, especially if claiming that every part of the broader administrative reform is complete.

### Climate, resilience, and transformation special fund

**Status: blocked.** The Senate approved a five-billion-euro establishment bill on 25 July 2023 and it was introduced as Drucksache 19/1099. A 26 March 2024 Senate report says that deliberations paused after the Federal Constitutional Court's 15 November 2023 judgment, pending a legal opinion.

The done-when requires the fund to be established by law with the promised initial five-billion-euro volume. It does not treat a cabinet decision or a bill as creation of the fund. Both programmes support special-fund investment tools, but the coalition switched them to a specifically climate-and-resilience fund, so adoption is `changed` rather than a clean takeover.

The bill and the Senate committee report are decisive primary records. The hard part is temporal: the pause report is from March 2024, so it cannot prove that the proposal never later resumed. A reviewer must check the complete PARDOK dossier, later budgets, and later Senate finance publications for a replacement measure or a formal withdrawal.

### European economic and technology location

**Status: not_assessable.** No status event is used because "one of the most important" European locations supplies neither a measure nor a comparator group. CDU's programme supported research-intensive technologies, and SPD's programme promised an internationally connected technology research location in Tegel.

No concrete done-when can be written without choosing a ranking, date, geography, or threshold that the agreement does not state. That would turn an aspirational claim into an invented target. The adoption is substantive, but it does not cure the lack of an assessable completion test.

The programme passages establish adoption. For publication, a person would need to confirm that the claim remains intentionally `not_assessable`, rather than silently importing an index or investment target from elsewhere.

### Federal Council initiative on threats against witnesses and court personnel

**Status: implemented.** Bundesrat Drucksache 449/24 is a "Gesetzesantrag des Landes Berlin" and records the Senate decision to send a draft amending the Criminal Code and Code of Criminal Procedure on threats against witnesses and court personnel to the Federal Council.

The done-when is the submission of the named initiative, not enactment of federal criminal law. This distinction is material: Berlin can initiate a Bundesrat measure, but cannot itself amend the StGB. CDU's programme contains a related Federal Council criminal-law initiative, but not this witness-threat proposal; no matching SPD programme passage was found. The coalition commitment therefore changes the programme commitment's subject while retaining the instrument.

The Federal Council printed matter is decisive and is a PDF. A reviewer should check the committee, Bundesrat, Bundestag, and final-law stages if publication discusses what happened after the initiative. It would be misleading to imply that the federal criminal-law amendment itself took effect.

## Where the draft model did not fit cleanly

- `implemented` is slightly awkward for the Federal Council item. Berlin fully performed its own procedural promise by submitting the initiative, while the eventual legal result is outside Berlin's competence. The model would benefit from an explicit distinction between completing a state-controlled procedural commitment and obtaining the federal outcome.
- The steel-boat item has two missed annual deadlines and a documented preparatory step, but there is no status for "behind schedule". `announced` records the actual preparation without pretending the annual delivery occurred. A deadline-missed event or field would make this much clearer.
- `blocked` fits the special fund because the Senate directly links its pause to a court judgment, but the evidence is a point-in-time report. The model has no field for a status's "last confirmed" date, which is important for a pause that might later end.
- Programme-to-coalition adoption is not always binary. The special-fund programmes support the instrument, not the named fund or amount. The witness item retains a Federal Council route while changing the criminal-law subject. `changed` is usable but the record would benefit from a short adoption rationale as a structured field.
- The administration commitment contains both a legal replacement and broader operational aspirations. Splitting it into a legal promise and a service-improvement promise would prevent a passed statute from being read as proof that administrative performance improved.

## Berlin documentation system and review needs

PARDOK's captured documents use stable direct-PDF patterns such as `https://pardok.parlament-berlin.de/starweb/adis/citat/VT/19/SchrAnfr/S19-22511.pdf` for a written question and `https://www.parlament-berlin.de/ados/19/IIIPlen/vorgang/d19-2353.pdf` for a printed matter. The public PARDOK browser and the parliamentary proceedings overview are the discovery surfaces; direct documents are PDFs. Searching needs both the promise wording and administrative synonyms: the administration reform appears as `Verwaltungsstrukturreformgesetz`, `Landesorganisationsgesetz`, and `LOG`, not only as `AZG`.

The most useful source in this trial was a written government answer. Bills and gazettes establish formal steps, while a question answer can state that an expected purchase did not happen. The budget is large and the relevant Water Police title is not named after the coalition promise, which makes direct text search and human context review necessary. The Federal Council material is outside PARDOK and sits in the Bundestag/Bundesrat document service, also as PDFs.

Before any publication, a reviewer should verify the exact programme-to-coalition links, assess the administration law's transitional provisions, look for later evidence on the climate fund and boat procurement, and separate Berlin's completed submission of the federal initiative from the federal legislature's result. Every listed status remains a proposed AI judgement pending that human review.
